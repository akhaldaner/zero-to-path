# Philosophy

Architect, not user. Teach before auditing. Build understanding in layers, not in a hurry.

## The core idea

An engineer is not someone who knows a language. An engineer is someone who understands the machine well enough to build with it — and honest enough to know what they do not yet understand.

This project treats learning as engineering: measurable, reproducible, verifiable. Reading is not learning. Watching is not learning. Only reconstruction from memory, done by hand, verified through practice, and explained in writing to the mentor counts.

Mastery is not a feeling. It is a procedural state. A topic becomes `mastered` only when a subject mentor verifies the learner's independent work, submits a petition, and a dispatcher confirms the result into the knowledge map. The knowledge map is the source of truth. Notes carry material and evidence trails; they do not invent status.

## Teaching before auditing

The AI mentor is a teacher-guide, not an inspector.

Its first job is to make the mechanism visible: what something is, why it exists, how it behaves, where it breaks, and how an engineer checks it. Its second job is to verify that the learner can reproduce the understanding independently.

A good mentor gives light before asking for proof.

It does not hide necessary explanation behind “figure it out yourself”. It does not demand in practice what it did not teach in theory. It does not turn a note into homework. It does not turn a drill into an infinite audit.

Strictness remains, but it becomes fair:

```text
Explain first.
Then verify.
Require only what was taught.
Do not punish gaps that normal teaching could have closed.
```

## The T-shaped principle

Two axes, one person:

- Deep vertical — backend engineering: Python, SQL, APIs, Linux, system design.
- Wide horizontal — the systems layer underneath: memory model, C++, CUDA, linear algebra.

Both are built in parallel, over years, until they meet at the level where high-level services touch physical hardware.

## Order matters

Temptation says: start with GPU programming now — it is exciting. Discipline says: become a backend engineer first, then descend into systems.

Backend first — that is where the market is, and where the resources for the deeper journey come from.

C++ second — once working as an engineer, learn how memory and CPU really behave.

CUDA and AI infrastructure last — the hardest layer, approached from stability rather than survival.

Mixing these layers too early is how students stall for years. Order is not a preference — it is strategy.

## Balance, not sprinting

Every subject moves every week. Mathematics feeds Computer Science. Computer Science explains what happens under Python. Linux shows the machine Python runs on. English opens access to everything else.

A week of “only Python” is not progress — it is drift. A skipped subject for two days is a signal to review the schedule, not to double the next session.

## Repetition beats volume

Knowledge that cannot be recalled is knowledge that does not exist.

Every topic passes through independent reconstruction, verification, written answers to the mentor, and error analysis before it can be called `mastered`. Written explanation is mandatory. Oral practice may be used as personal reinforcement, but it is not a system gate.

Spaced review and warm-up recall are not decoration. They keep closed topics from silently rotting.

## Notes are maps, not prisons

A `NOTE` is a full explanatory learning artifact. It should remove confusion, not create artificial gaps. It explains mechanism before formula, syntax, command, or rule. It defines new terms when they first appear. It shows worked examples, boundaries, and typical traps.

But a `NOTE` is not a worksheet. It does not become the place where mastery is proven. Practice, testing, prediction, error labs, and written answers belong to `DRL`, `UNT`, or `EXM`.

Curiosity is not converted into debt. Material above the current required level may live in a note as `Backlog / полезно позже` — useful later, not mandatory now.

## Evidence is sufficient, not perfect

`mastered` does not mean flawless prose, perfect style, or exhaustive coverage.

It means sufficient verifiable evidence:

- the learner worked independently;
- the task or test was completed;
- the result was verified in a way appropriate to the subject;
- the learner could explain the mechanism in writing;
- errors were analysed where applicable;
- the required apparatus did not exceed the current level;
- the sequence of learning was not violated.

A drill is a micro-proof, not an interrogation. A mentor may reject work only through a concrete blocking criterion and a minimum fix. Infinite revision cycles are a process defect, not rigor.

## Small steps, not heroism

The plan fails the day it depends on heroism. Compensating a missed block at 2 a.m. breaks tomorrow. Cutting sleep to fit more study reduces tomorrow’s capacity.

If a block is missed — it is marked and rescheduled, not absorbed at the cost of rest. Rest, movement, and sleep are part of the plan, not obstacles to it.

## Documents as engineering artifacts

Notes are not an archive. They are a second brain, and they must be maintained like code: structured, linked, versioned by stable artifact IDs, and honest about what is and is not yet verified.

A note that does not connect to a knowledge map does not exist. A `mastered` status without dispatcher confirmation is not valid.

The system serves learning, not reporting. The learner keeps one daily log. Mentors and dispatchers generate petitions, responses, and handover snapshots only when needed.

## The dispatcher as navigator, not second examiner

The dispatcher does not duplicate the subject mentor’s work.

The mentor checks understanding. The dispatcher checks the system: sequence, debts, balance, current level, petition validity, process defects, and readiness for phase transitions.

By default, the dispatcher reads the daily log and petitions. It does not reread full practice artifacts unless an audit trigger appears. `Dispatcher_state` is a handover snapshot, not a daily bureaucratic ritual.

## The project as an applied polygon

The pet project is not a separate escape route. Financial Navigator, short FinG, is a Phase 0 applied polygon where Python, Linux, Git, and later math and CS meet a real product. It must not outrun the learner’s current foundation.

FinG has no knowledge map and no `mastered` status. Its progress lives in the repository: roadmap, commits, working code, and decisions.

## What this is not

- Not a race.
- Not a shortcut.
- Not a template for “learn to code in 90 days”.
- Not a claim that everyone should do this.
- Not a system that confuses reporting with learning.
- Not an audit regime disguised as education.

It is one experiment, run carefully, with all cards on the table.
